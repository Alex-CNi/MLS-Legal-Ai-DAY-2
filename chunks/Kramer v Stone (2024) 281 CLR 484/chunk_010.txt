The difference between a mere incomplete gift – “I give you
property X” – and a case such as this – “I will, in the future, give you
property X” – is that, unlike a mere incomplete gift, a promise to give
property in the future has ongoing force. The continuation of the
promise is “conduct of the defendant after the representation in
encouraging the plaintiff to act upon it” (22).
Also contrary to the appellants’ submissions are the reasons of
Brennan J in Waltons Stores (Interstate) Ltd v Maher (23). The
appellants read Brennan J’s third and fourth “elements” – “(3) the
plaintiff acts or abstains from acting in reliance on the assumption or
expectation”; “(4) the defendant knew or intended him to do so” – out
of context of his Honour’s reasons as a whole. [He quoted Brennan J
in Waltons Stores (Interstate) Ltd v Maher (24).] It is plain that
Brennan J’s references to knowledge and intention were to the
knowledge and intention of the promisor at the time of making the
promise, not later knowledge or intention directed to the plaintiff’s acts
of reliance. The Court of Appeal correctly understood the primary
