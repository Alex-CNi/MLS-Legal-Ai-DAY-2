focus is only upon an equitable estoppel which arises by reason of
encouragement from a promise, the elements that must be satisﬁed can
be reﬁned as follows.
First, as with the requirement for representations in estoppels by
representation generally (65), there must be a “clear and unequivocal”
promise made by the party estopped (the promisor) to the party who
relies upon the promise (the promisee) (66). Of its nature, the clear and
unequivocal promise will generally concern some representation about
future conduct (67).
Secondly, a reasonable person in the promisor’s position must have
expected or intended (or the promisor actually did expect or intend)
that the promisee would rely upon the promise by some action,
omission or course of conduct (68). This is what is meant by
references, including throughout this appeal, to the promise being an
“encouragement” for the promisee to act.
Thirdly, the promisee must have relied upon the promise by acting
or omitting to act in the general manner that would have been
expected. In order to establish reliance, it is ordinarily necessary for
the promisee to show not merely that the promise was one factor taken
into account in motivating the promisee’s action or omission but that
the promisee would not have acted or omitted to act in the absence of
the promise (69). In other words, it must usually be shown that the
promisee’s reliance “made a difference to [the promisee] taking the
course of action or inaction” (70).
