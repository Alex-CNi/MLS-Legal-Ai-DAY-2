The second contention does not arise on the case brought by
Mr Stone, which was based on estoppel by encouragement, and not by
acquiescence (115). Neither actual nor constructive knowledge on
Dame Leonie’s part that Mr Stone had relied upon her promise to his
detriment would have supported an estoppel by encouragement. As
explained by Leeming JA, the relevant state of mind for an estoppel by
encouragement is an intention on Dame Leonie’s part to induce
Mr Stone to adopt the assumption that he would inherit the farm (116).
There was no ﬁnding that Dame Leonie had that state of mind when
she made the promise (117).
Proprietary estoppel based upon encouragement
A proprietary estoppel is a species of equitable estoppel; that is, it is
an equity created by estoppel (118). Like other equitable estoppels, its
object is “to prevent an unjust departure by one person from an
assumption adopted by another as the basis of some act or omission
which, unless the assumption be adhered to, would operate to that
other’s detriment” (119). Whether a party will be bound by a promise
depends on the part taken by that party in occasioning its adoption
by the other party (120). It is a promisor’s responsibility for the
promisee’s detrimental reliance upon the promise which gives rise to
an estoppel (121).

estoppel against them has variously been described as “fraudu-
